Landing page: one page, A/B test on the hero

All traffic (ads, emails, organic) goes to one landing page. Unbounce splits visitors 50/50 between two heroes; everything below the hero stays identical. aol_variant records which hero each visitor saw (a or b), all the way to the Salesforce order.

ArmHeroTrafficaol_variant
AThe current hero: "Stop Waking Up at 3 AM", the photo reel, the participant line50%a
BOne change from A, to be decided. The ready candidate is the headline "Switch Off the Racing Mind", which speaks to the Wired & Tired audience50%b

How to read it. At ~2,000–4,000 sessions for the whole flight, the test can't pick a winner on registrations; that would need about 12,000–14,000 visitors per arm. Judge it instead on the hero button's click-through to course select (the aol_cta event), which happens far more often. At a ~20% click rate, spotting a lift to 25% takes about 1,100 visitors per arm, which the January budget reaches. Registrations by arm are reported too, as a check.

Change one thing. The old page's test varied six things at once and couldn't explain its result. Hero B changes one element: the headline, or the image, not both.

Key findings

1
AOL holds an empty spot in the market.

Almost every alternative is self-serve (apps, supplements, devices) or long and expensive (MBSR $450–650 over 8 weeks, TM about $980). We found no other live, instructor-led, sleep-specific program under about $300. The closest substitutes are coached CBT-I programs: Sleep Reset ($297, HSA/FSA) and Stellar Sleep (about $60 a month).

2
Buyers already accept the method.

Meditation and breathwork are the most-tried sleep trend in the US (27% of adults, AASM, Dec 2025), ahead of sleepmaxxing (12%) and mouth taping (7%). AOL doesn't need to explain why breathing helps. It needs to explain why a live teacher is worth more than a free YouTube yoga nidra.

3
People describe the problem as a busy mind, and the core buyer is a woman aged 30–55.

63% of people who want more sleep are frequently stressed, and only 27% of women under 50 get enough sleep (Gallup). The fastest-growing supplement sales are calming ingredients: magnesium is up 33.5% and L-theanine 24%.

4
The tired messages are scores, percentages and pills.

Skepticism of melatonin is rising, 76% of adults have lost sleep worrying about their sleep, and only about 1 in 10 trust most health influencers (Pew 2026). A calm, human "skill you keep, not something you take" stands apart.

5
Meta's health-data rules are the biggest risk, and they affect ad buying more than copy.

A pixel classified as health-related can lose Purchase optimization. "Anxiety" in the URL and the HSA/FSA pitch are possible triggers.

Our own participant survey New · Nov 18

From the national program coordinator: Art of Living's pre/post survey of Sleep & Anxiety Protocol participants, April 2026. Matched pairs; anxiety measured on the full State-Trait Anxiety Inventory (STAI-S); paired t-tests and effect sizes. Not peer reviewed, no control group, and the sleep questions are custom self-report items. It is the only data on this course itself.

1
Waking refreshed was the biggest change.

More than half of participants reported sleeping better on all three measures (falling asleep, staying asleep, waking refreshed), all statistically significant. Morning refreshedness moved most. That is direct evidence for the campaign's core idea: sell the morning, not the night.

2
Anxiety came down, mostly as calm.

66% scored lower on the STAI after the course (medium effect size). 60% of those who arrived with frequent anxiety moved to a lower category. Physical tension and worry fell, and feelings of calm and ease rose. That fits the "busy mind" message for the Wired & Tired audience.

3
People recommend it.

8.85 out of 10 likelihood to recommend, a Net Promoter Score of 57. This is a social-proof number that needs no testimonial consent and makes no health claim.

Cleared for use. The national program coordinator confirmed on Nov 18 that all findings can be used. The rulings above still apply: they are about what each claim can support, not permission. Worth asking for, but not blocking:

  • How many participants (matched pairs) and how many cohorts? Adding "n = X" to the source line makes every claim stronger.
  • When was the post-survey taken? End of day 3, or later? It changes whether we can say "after the course" or "weeks later."
  • The exact percentage for each sleep item, so ads can say "X% woke more refreshed" instead of "more than half."
  • Response rate, and whether these were US online cohorts.
  • Any follow-up data (30 or 90 days), which would be the strongest claim available.

Counsel should review the ad lines with the HSA/FSA wording on Dec 4. Survey results describe what participants reported; they are not a promise of typical results.

  • Spending is modest per person. 51% spent $100 or less on sleep products (excluding mattresses) last year, and men are more likely to spend $200–500 (AASM 2025). $149 needs a comparison: about two years of an app, or a little over half of one private CBT-I session.

  • Apps don't hold January users. Calm and Headspace keep about 8% of new January users at 30 days (Apptopia; dated, but the most-cited figure).
  • The January / HSA-FSA window is real. An employee's whole FSA election (up to $3,400) is available on Jan 1, and HSAs hold $192B across 42.6M accounts. 3 AM isn't about the hours you lose. It's about the day you can't face afterwards. So we don't sell sleep. We sell the morning you get back.

Night appears only as a brief, abstract moment (a clock face, phone glow, open eyes) and never as a person suffering in bed. Morning gets most of the frame time in every asset. This keeps the brief's "3 AM vs 7 AM" concept within the brand guide's ban on dark bedroom imagery.

Claims and platform rules

  • Meta's personal-attributes rule. Never write "your anxiety," "struggling with insomnia?" or "can't sleep?" Use shared or third-person terms: "3 AM thoughts are loud."
  • No promises to cure insomnia or anxiety, nothing that suggests replacing medical care, and no "fall asleep in X minutes." Wherever research is cited, include the line: these studies examine the technique, not this course. Never cite the SKY research, since this course doesn't teach SKY.
  • No guilt, fear, manufactured urgency, countdowns without a real date, or exit popups.
  • Don't name competitors' products or ingredients (melatonin, magnesium, apps).
  • HSA/FSA always carries its asterisk, with wording confirmed by counsel (IRS IR-2024-65).
  • Testimonials run only with the consent schema. A stock photo never stands in for a real named person.
  • Google/YouTube: health-related creative loses remarketing, Customer Match and lookalike audiences. Plan YouTube as prospecting only.

Attribution policy

At around 100 purchases, modeled attribution is mostly noise. Each tool answers one question:

  • Salesforce orders: the source of truth. With UTMs stored on the order, this answers which ad, audience and variant produced each registration. The wrap report uses this.
  • HubSpot: email touches. Did a follow-up email bring the buyer back?
  • GA4: the funnel. Where people drop off, landing page conversion, and the split between audiences.
  • Meta and Google: budget signals only. Never reconciled one-to-one against GA4.
